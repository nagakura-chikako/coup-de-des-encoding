# 11 · Afterword

*This is the only page in this repository written in the first person, and the only one that is not normative. Nothing that follows constrains the record. I use the "I" that scholarly writing ordinarily avoids in order to make explicit that the party answerable here is a human being. The preceding documents say what this structure is not; this page says what it may become.*

---

The documentation before this page is almost entirely negative. It states what level‑0 refuses to contain, what the project is not, what a layer may not do. That was deliberate. A foundation meant to be shared is defined by its restraint, and the restraint is what makes it shareable. But a foundation defined only by its refusals has not yet said what it is for.

**It is not for fixing a reading.**

I have readings of this poem. Some I have published; others are still waiting, inside me, to be rewritten. None is in the canonical record, and the record is built so that even a scholar who finds every one of them wrong can use the whole of it. This is not modesty. It is the only arrangement under which the record is worth building: a foundation carrying my conclusions would be a foundation only for those who already share them.

Rather than enclosing a reading of my own, what I hope for is the layering of many kinds of work. Level‑0 is common ground — a set of addresses that stay where they are — and above it there is room for readings, for source materials, for references, for refutations, for hypotheses that will not survive their own decade. Two scholars who disagree completely can attach their disagreement to the same identifiers, and a third can see exactly where the two accounts part company, because both are anchored to the same points. Disagreement stops being a fork and becomes a comparison.

There is a second hope. **The traces of the work are worth keeping.** Not only the conclusions: the errors, the hesitations, the abandoned hypotheses, the questions that dissolved on closer inspection. An error can even prompt a discovery. Modern scholarly practice publishes its results while often discarding its process, and each generation then repeats the same wrong turns because nobody wrote them down. The structure here keeps the process, because keeping it costs almost nothing. A correction always carries its date, its author, and what was consulted. A retired identifier keeps a pointer to its successor. A question that turned out to be no question keeps its resolution rather than being tidied away. Someone should be able to see, years from now, not only what the record says but how it came to say it — and where it nearly said something else.

I would want that even if the record were complete and correct. It is neither, and that is why the guarantee matters. Level‑0 is itself a zero only provisionally: it holds just so long as one printed state — reset after the poet's death, and last only by accident — is taken as settled. The promise made here is not that the record is right. It is **that if the record is wrong, it cannot be wrong quietly.**

None of this is specific to Mallarmé, or to literature. Any field that must distinguish between what was observed and what was concluded from it meets the same difficulty, and usually meets it too late. In experimental work the measurement and its interpretation come to live in the same file, and by the time they need separating they cannot be. In industry, decisions are preserved while the evidence that produced them is not. In work on machine systems, outputs are assessed without a route back to what they rest on. I claim no expertise in those fields. I claim one thing only. **The problem I met in a poem printed in 1897 was a problem of literature, and was not a problem of literature.** It appeared within literature and did not stay within it. Today, the response to that Mallarméan problem will look for its form outside literature as well.

I want to record one experience that shaped the design. It was not, in the ordinary sense, a literary experience either — and yet for me it touched what literature essentially is.

Much of this encoding was drafted in conversation with machine assistants, and the conversation was genuinely useful; work that would have taken years advanced in a few weeks. It was also, early on, confidently and plausibly wrong three times — always in the same manner, filling in from prior familiarity what the page did not actually show. The photographs corrected it. What I took from this was not a caution about machines; I am capable of exactly that error, and made it myself. What I took was a lesson, and a requirement. **An output that cannot be checked is not knowledge, whoever produced it.** Fluency is not evidence of correctness. A claim that carries neither date nor author nor a route back to what it rests on does not become trustworthy by being well expressed. And this has to hold in both directions: my assertions must be verifiable by machines and by other scholars, and machine assertions must be verifiable by me, by other people, and by other machines. This is not a demand invented for a new kind of collaborator. It is the demand scholarship has always made of itself, and it does not become droppable because the sentences have grown smooth.

So the structure is built to outlive its makers, human and otherwise. Everything in it can be contradicted; nothing in it can be contradicted anonymously.

The layers will overtake me. I hope some of them refute me. **The work that saw furthest and deepest, and found a form for what it saw, will leave the future the most room to work in.** What is left to the rest of us is to prepare good ground and keep the addresses steady. As something one can do for someone we will never meet again — or never meet even once — that is not a small thing.
